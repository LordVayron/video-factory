# Story architect (the writers' room)

**The script is the product.** Pretty footage of a dull script is a dull video. The standard: every line earns its place, the dialogue has character and attitude, and the video works even muted.

Read `references/comedy.md` before writing anything funny. Read `references/storytelling.md` for non-comedy formats.

## Process (fast: ~5 minutes of writing, one approval)
1. **Premises (private).** Write 10 one-line "what if" premises for the characters and format. Each = base reality + one unusual thing. Kill anything generic ("X does a performance review"). Keep the 3 with the most specific, visual, character-driven game.
2. **Find the game.** For the best premise, state it in one sentence: "The game is: ___." List 5 heightening moves ("if this is true, what else is true?"). Keep the 3 that escalate, and order them so the biggest is last.
3. **Draft fast.** Full script with no pausing. Placeholders (`****`) are fine.
4. **Punch-up pass.** For every punchline, write 3–5 alternatives and keep the funniest. Check word choice: the specific, funnier word (yardstick, not ruler). Put the funniest word last. Cut every line that only explains or sets up without a turn.
5. **Character pass.** Each line must only be sayable by that character (for example: the schemer is precise, corporate, wounded pride; the sidekick is literal, joyful, wrong for the right reasons; the deflator is dry and sees through everyone). Swap the names: if a line still works, rewrite it.
6. **Visual pass.** The first 1.5 s works as a freeze-frame, every beat has a visible action, and the ending reads with the sound off.
7. **Score it** (`references/comedy.md` rubric). Under 8 average or any criterion under 7: rewrite before showing.
8. **Scene and blocking plan.** Group shots into scenes (one location and moment each, ≤ 15 s, 2–5 shots of 2–5 s). Per scene, write the blocking sheet: where everyone sits or stands, which way they face, what they hold, and where they move (and when). This text goes into every storyboard and video prompt.

## What the user sees (the only writing gate)
A single message with:
- **Logline** and **the game** (one line each).
- **The script** as a table read: `SHOT · who · line`, with stage directions in *italics*.
- **The 2 alternative punchlines** for the main laugh and the button, so they can pick.
- **Runtime** and **estimated cost**.
No images exist yet. Revise until approved. Then hand the plan to `cinematographer`.

## Hard rules
- Hook in 0–1.5 s: an image that makes the viewer ask a question.
- Beats join with "but" or "therefore", never "and then".
- No line restates what we can see. No "As you know…". No jokes explained after they land.
- Dialogue lines are ≤ 12 words and sound spoken. People interrupt, deflect and answer the wrong question.
- The button tops the biggest laugh, or flips it. Never end on a line that merely summarizes.
- Spanish: write natively in Mexican Spanish (no Spain-isms); never translate a joke literally.
