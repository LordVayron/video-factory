# Vertical DNA: micro-drama (vertical "novela" series for TikTok, Reels and Shorts)

Serialized melodrama told in 60–120-second vertical episodes: secret billionaires, revenge, forbidden love, betrayal, hidden identity. **Every episode ends on a hook that makes the next one a must-watch.** Emotion beats subtlety.

## What makes it work

- **Hook in the first 2 seconds:** open mid-conflict, with a slap, a reveal, a humiliation or a line like "You're fired." No establishing shots.
- **One turn per episode, and a cliffhanger at the end.** The last 5 seconds deliver a reveal, a threat or a question: the ring is fake, the CEO is her ex, the baby isn't his.
- **Big, readable emotions.** Close-ups on faces, tears, clenched jaws, and reaction shots held a beat longer than feels natural. Viewers watch with sound off half the time, so the face has to tell it.
- **Archetypes the audience knows instantly:** the humiliated underdog with a secret, the arrogant rival, the cold powerful love interest, the scheming relative. Set them up in one line each.
- **Satisfaction loops:** the underdog is wronged, then gets payback ("face-slapping" scenes). Deliver one payback every 2–3 episodes to keep viewers paying off the tension.
- **Consistency is the format.** The same faces, wardrobe and sets across 20–80 episodes. Lock every character sheet and set plate in the bible before episode 1.

## Structure

**Series:** a premise in one sentence (who, the secret, the injustice, the promised payback), 3–5 main characters, 2–4 sets, and an arc of 20–40 episodes in acts of 8–10.

**Episode (60–120 s):**
1. Recap-free cold open on conflict (0–3 s).
2. Escalation: the antagonist pushes, the hero endures (3–40 s).
3. Turn: new information or a decision (40–80 s).
4. Cliffhanger: a reveal or a threat, ending on a held reaction close-up plus a sting (last 5 s).

## Writing rules

- Dialogue is short, declarative and quotable: "From today, this company is mine."
- Each scene has one clear power shift. Write it as "X has power → Y takes it".
- Subtext is spelled out on faces and in short lines. No long speeches.
- Captions are always on, large, in the safe zone, in the series language (Spanish micro-dramas use neutral Latin American Spanish unless the creator wants a local flavor).
- **Length:** 60–120 s per episode, about 150 wpm, 8–15 shots.

## Production settings

- **Format:** 9:16, 720p, `series/<id>/` for the cast, sets, intro and voices (same mechanism as any series).
- **Cast:** realistic, attractive, clearly distinct faces (hair color, silhouette, wardrobe color code per character). Original characters only, never real actors.
- **Pipeline:** the same as the sitcom: script, radio cut, storyboard grids, Production Book, one multi-shot generation per scene, continuity, edit. The edit uses no laugh track. It uses music stings on turns, a dramatic swell under the cliffhanger, and a freeze on the last reaction.
- **Video model:** Seedance 2 family at 720p (strong faces and consistency). Close-ups dominate, which helps identity hold.
- **Look:** glossy, high-contrast, warm skin tones, luxury interiors or office settings. The "cinematic-hyperreal" style sheet is a good base.

## Pacing QC

| Metric | Pass |
|---|---|
| Conflict on screen by | 3 s |
| Average shot length | 2–5 s |
| Reaction close-ups | at least 1 per power shift |
| Cliffhanger | in the last 5 s, ends on a held face + sting |
| Captions | on, legible, in the safe zone |
| Character identity across episodes | same face, hair and wardrobe code |
